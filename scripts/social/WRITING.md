# Writing X posts: @bitcoinweighin

The brief for the daily slates (`slates/<date>.ts`). Read it before drafting.
Replies to big accounts have their own brief: `../radar/STYLE.md`.

It draws on what our own posts have done since launch, and on an outside
"viral ghostwriting" guide (Oct 2026). From that guide we kept the mechanics that
work with true numbers and dropped everything that needs false ones.

## Hard rules (never traded for reach)

- **Every number is real**: from the live data or a sourced figure, correct at
  the close it names, and checkable on the site. Never exaggerate or round up to
  provoke replies. The site is a measuring instrument. One fake number caught
  costs more than any viral post earns.
- **No invented people, stories, quotes or dialogue.** Quote real people only
  from the record ("cyber Manhattan" is Saylor's own phrase).
- No fake scarcity ("deleting soon"), no follow-gated giveaways, no
  "am I the only one who…" status anxiety, no price predictions.
- No profanity, no hashtags, and at most one emoji.
- Caveats stay (illustrative prices, the 2014 land value) but go on the card,
  in the reply or on the last line, never in the hook.

## What our own posts say (to 6 Oct 2026)

- **Best:** Saylor's "cyber Manhattan" line with Strategy's coins filling the
  real island (video), then the Moon ride (video). Each pairs a famous name or
  an absurd scale with motion.
- **Worst:** routine lines like "Today's Weigh-In: 1 BTC = 20.4 oz of gold".
  They get single-digit views. A number with no tension is just a price ticker.
- Fast replies under big accounts' posts reach more people than any post of ours.

## The hook (first line)

- **Lead with the surprise, not the setup.**
  ✗ "El Salvador buys about one bitcoin a day."
  ✓ "El Salvador's bitcoin, in $1 bills, would leave the stratosphere."
- **Break an expectation in the first six to eight words**, with data, not
  opinion: "Gold was meant to be the safe one this year."
- **Put one number in the hook that people will remember.** The rest can follow.
- **Real names pull:** Saylor, Schiff, Bukele, BlackRock, Satoshi, the US
  government. Use their own words only when they're on the record.
- **Write for someone who isn't a bitcoiner.** If it needs an ounce of jargon
  to land, simplify it until it doesn't.

## Patterns that fit our data

1. **Do the actual math.** "People say X. Here's the math." Show two or three
   lines a reader can check in their head, then a conclusion that cuts against
   X. That's the whole site in one post.
2. **Same money, different weight.** One amount, two things, wildly different
   sizes: gold in a pocket, silver on a trolley.
3. **Tribal split.** Gold bugs vs bitcoiners, silver vs gold, the ETF vs
   Strategy. Both sides should feel right and want to say so. Be fair to both:
   show bitcoin's bad stretches too.
4. **Guess first.** Ask the question and put the answer in the image or video:
   "El Salvador's bitcoin in $1 bills. How tall? Guess before you look." The
   replies are guesses, and the picture pays them off. It's a curiosity gap
   that hides nothing.
5. **A scale people can feel:** the Moon, the stratosphere, Everest, a
   Manhattan block, a pocket, a suitcase, a trolley.

## Rhythm

- Short lines, with a blank line every one to three lines.
- Vary the length: a long line of numbers, then a three-word punch ("Bring a
  trolley.").
- Plain words. Write like a person, not a dashboard.

## How X ranks it

X's ranking weights, published in 2023, scored a reply at about 27 times a
like, and a reply that the author answers at about 150 times. "Show less of
this" and reports scored heavily negative. The weights have probably changed
since, but the direction holds:

- End on something people want to answer: a guess, or a side to take. Not
  "thoughts?".
- Answer replies on our posts, quickly.
- Rage bait that gets us muted costs more than it earns.
- X demotes links in the post, so the link goes in the first reply (`lib.ts`
  does this).

## From outside guides: not used, and why

- Exaggerated numbers, invented anecdotes, fake scarcity, age and income
  flexes, lead magnets: we sell nothing, and being right is our only asset.
- The 40/30/20/10 content mix is built for selling services.
- "Show more" cut-offs only exist for long posts, which need X Premium. `lib.ts`
  keeps posts to 280 characters. If the account gets Premium, a cut right
  after the hook is worth testing.

## Before a post goes in a slate

- Would someone stop on the first line?
- Is there one number they'll remember?
- Could a gold bug and a bitcoiner both reply?
- Is every figure from the data, and correct at the close it names?
- Does the image or video show the answer?
